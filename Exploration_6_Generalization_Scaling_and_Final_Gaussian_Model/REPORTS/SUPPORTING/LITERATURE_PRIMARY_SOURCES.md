# Targeted primary-source rationale

Sources checked during the Exploration6 study. Literature motivates bounded hypotheses; it does not override local executed evidence or authorize a new search grid.

1. Bingyi Kang et al., *Decoupling Representation and Classifier for Long-Tailed Recognition*, ICLR2020. The work studies representation learning separately from classifier balancing on long-tail benchmarks. It motivates the distinct cRT hypothesis, rather than another stacked class-weighting loss. This PUMA experiment uses the certified W/R reset and Ph/Pb freeze implementation; it is not a claim to reproduce the original paper's entire recipe. The local cRT controls failed the ROI improvement guard despite reducing the semantic train–DEV gap. [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.09217).

2. David Tellez et al., *Quantifying the effects of data augmentation and stain color normalization in convolutional neural networks for computational pathology*, Medical Image Analysis2019. The authors compare augmentation/normalization on pathology data spanning four organs and nine laboratories. This supports testing stain variation as a plausible generalization mechanism. It does not establish that the fixed H/E perturbation improves a frozen UNI2-h nucleus classifier; that remains the purpose of the ongoing matched AUG0/AUG1/AUG2 study. [Author paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06543).

3. Gavin Cawley and Nicola Talbot, *On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias in Performance Evaluation*, JMLR2010. The analysis motivates separating model selection from final performance estimation. Here the operational consequence is bounded HPO inside TRAIN-only grouped folds, at most two DEV finalists, and recipe freeze before the two final holdouts. This is a methodological inference applied to the present design, not evidence that ROI grouping establishes patient-level independence. [Journal article](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html).
